# Mechanism: where PTQ damage lands, and the interpretability toolkit

Within-language predictors of damage (confidence, entropy, residual norm), the data-size debate, and the methods (logit lens, patching, language dimensions) reused in [../design.md](../project/design.md) §5. Back to [../papers.md](index.md).

### Proskurina 2024. When Quantization Affects Confidence of Large Language Models?
- File: `papers/Proskurina-2024-When-Quantization-Affects-Confidence-LLMs.pdf`
- Venue: Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: NAACL 2024, pages 1918–1928
- Does: Quantizes BLOOM (560M–7.1B), OPT (125M–13B), Mistral-7B and LLaMA-7B with 4-bit GPTQ (128 C4 samples, group 128) and compares full vs quantized predictive probabilities on six English zero-shot multiple-choice tasks (ARC-E, BoolQ, PIQA, HellaSwag, OBQA, XStory); measures accuracy, ECE/ACE, mean confidence in the true class, predictive entropy and Jensen–Shannon distance.
- Finds: Confidence in the true class drops significantly after GPTQ for all four ~7B models (paired t-test, e.g. LLaMA 56.8 -> 53.7 on HellaSwag) and calibration error mostly rises (LLaMA-7B ~10% higher CE on HellaSwag). Binning by pre-quantization confidence shows the largest confidence shifts on samples where the full model was already unsure; over-confident samples barely move. JSD between full and quantized true-class distributions shrinks with scale (except LLaMA).
- Use here: Direct precedent for E1/E2 (thin knowledge -> low pre-quantization confidence -> larger damage); reuse its per-sample "confidence in gold answer" and entropy measures and its confidence-bin analysis, applied per language instead of per benchmark.
- Note: English only, one quantization method, one calibration set, no confidence intervals on the bin plot; the authors themselves suggest extending to token-level distributions, which is what this project does.

### Chang 2025. Why Do Some Inputs Break Low-Bit LLM Quantization?
- File: `papers/Chang-2025-Why-Some-Inputs-Break-Low-Bit-Quantization.pdf`
- Venue: Proceedings of the 2025 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing (EMNLP 2025), pages 3410–3429
- Does: Quantizes Qwen2.5-7B, Llama3-8B, Mistral-Nemo-12B and Llama3-70B with 3–4-bit weight-only GPTQ, AWQ, NormalFloat and EfficientQAT and defines per-example quantization error as the increase in mean NLL on 10k English FineWeb documents (512 tokens each); then uses early exiting (logit lens: project the residual state r^(l) through the final RMSNorm and unembedding) and cross-model activation patching (copy full-precision hattn, hgate, hup or hdown outputs into the 3-bit model in the upper half of the layers) to localise where errors arise.
- Measure: residual magnitude = mean over tokens of the L2 norm of the post-attention, pre-MLP residual state r^(l) in the full-precision model; the correlation with final error becomes increasingly negative in upper layers and is strongest at the last layer L. Direction: SMALL residual norms break, not large ones (final-layer Pearson correlation with 3-bit error averages −0.66 / −0.76 / −0.78 / −0.80 for the four models).
- Finds: Errors of 50 method pairs correlate at avg rho 0.82 (Qwen2.5-7B lowest, 0.66), so which inputs break is largely fixed by the full-precision model. Small-norm residuals have fewer outliers (lower kurtosis), and RMSNorm reverses the ordering so their post-norm MLP inputs are larger and amplify weight error over layers; large-error examples rely on late layers to fix their predictions (sharp NLL drop after ~layer 36/40 in Mistral-12B), yet keeping the top 7 layers in 16-bit barely helps (ppl 8.67 -> 8.05 vs 5.60 FP), whereas patching MLP-gate outputs (or their inputs) recovers most of the gap. Long-tail (high-NLL) examples are hurt less, not more, and mid-popularity PopQA entities lose most (>10 points).
- Use here: The methodological template for the interpretability side: per-token/per-language residual-norm vs damage correlation, logit-lens divergence layer, and patching hgate/hdown to test E3-style activation mis-scaling; its long-tail result is a warning that E1/E2 cannot be assumed from rarity alone and must be measured per token.
- Note: English web text only; single calibration set per method; the norm-vs-error direction is specific to RMSNorm models (Llama, Qwen), so check it separately on the from-scratch GPT-2 (LayerNorm).

### Lotfi 2026. Quantized Reasoning Models Think They Need to Think Longer, but They Do Not
- File: `papers/Lotfi-2026-Quantized-Reasoning-Models-Think-Longer.pdf`
- Venue: preprint, arXiv:2606.00206 (v1, dated June 2, 2026; Meta FAIR)
- Does: Quantizes five reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen 1.5B/7B/14B, Distill-Llama 8B, QwQ-32B) with GPTQ and AWQ at 3/4-bit (g128) and FlatQuant W4A4KV4/W8A8KV8, evaluates on GSM8K, MATH-500, AIME, LiveCodeBench, GPQA-Diamond, and measures token-level KL(p_FP || q_quant) under identical generation prefixes.
- Finds: 3-bit AWQ cuts Qwen-1.5B MATH-500 accuracy 85.6 -> 47.0% while CoT grows 5.2K -> 23.4K tokens; overthinking rises from 19 of 72 errors (BF16) to 139 of 265 (52%). Position-level KL correlates with the full-precision model's next-token entropy at Spearman 0.92, and the highest-KL tokens are hesitation markers ("Wait", "But"); a fixed logit penalty on 50 such tokens shortens CoT by 12–23% with accuracy preserved or improved.
- Use here: Defines the per-position KL-vs-entropy analysis that E1/E2 predicts (quantization noise flips predictions where the full model is uncertain, i.e. small top-1/top-2 margin); adopt the same identical-prefix KL protocol and margin framing for the per-token damage measures.
- Note: English math/code/science only; sampling at T=0.6; reported spreads are over the penalty strength, not over seeds; the margin argument (Sec. 6) is offered as intuition, not a full causal account.

### Kumar 2024. Scaling Laws for Precision
- File: `papers/Kumar-2025-Scaling-Laws-For-Precision.pdf`
- Venue: ICLR 2025 (per iclr.cc listing; the PDF is arXiv:2411.04330 v2, dated 2024, no venue printed)
- Does: Trains 465 OLMo-style models (30–220M non-embedding parameters, 1.5–26B Dolma tokens, plus validation runs to 1.7B) in 3–16-bit precision and post-train quantizes them with GPTQ (two other methods in App. F), fitting loss-degradation scaling laws in N, D and bit-width.
- Finds: PTQ degradation follows delta_PTQ = C_T (D^gamma_D / N^gamma_N) exp(−P_post/gamma_post) (R^2 0.97): it grows with training tokens, shrinks with model size and rises exponentially as bits drop, so over-trained models can get worse after quantization with more data. Precision acts like an effective parameter count N_eff = N(1 − e^(−P/gamma)); models trained in lower precision degrade less under PTQ; compute-optimal training precision is ~7–8 bits.
- Use here: Background for E1/E2 in the "how much did the model learn about this language" sense: the from-scratch GPT-2 can vary tokens per language and test whether degradation scales with per-language exposure the way it scales with D/N here (which would predict LESS damage for under-exposed languages, the opposite of naive E1/E2).
- Note: Filename says 2025 but the PDF is dated 2024; small models, loss only, no downstream tasks; Catalan-Tatjer 2026 argues the data-scaling trend is largely a learning-rate-schedule confound.

### Ouyang 2025. Low-Bit Quantization Favors Undertrained LLMs
- File: `papers/Ouyang-2025-Low-Bit-Quantization-Favors-Undertrained-LLMs.pdf`
- Venue: Proceedings of the 63rd Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics (Volume 1: Long Papers), ACL 2025, pages 32338–32348
- Does: Quantizes 1500+ Pythia checkpoints (160M–12B, 20 checkpoints each up to ~206B tokens) with GPTQ at 2/3/4-bit (AWQ and bitsandbytes as checks) and fits quantization-induced degradation QiD = loss_q − loss_16 on 1,000 RefinedWeb texts as a function of tokens D, parameters N and bit-width P.
- Finds: QiD = 0.017 D^0.525 / (N^0.226 P^5.50): degradation grows with training tokens and shrinks with size and bits; 3-bit is near-lossless for the 12B model up to ~10^11 tokens then degrades, small models degrade much earlier. Extrapolation says a 70B model needs >17T tokens before 4-bit QiD exceeds 0.2; late-training weights change very little between checkpoints, so rounding pushes them outside their recent range.
- Use here: Same role as Kumar: an "undertrained = robust" prior that competes with E1/E2, and a design cue (they count tokens with one fixed tokenizer, Llama-3, so QiD is comparable across models, which matters for E4-style cross-language comparisons).
- Note: Single-stage Pythia pretraining, loss-only, English test data; Catalan-Tatjer 2026 shows the token-count trend is confounded by learning-rate decay when checkpoints are taken mid-schedule.

### Catalan-Tatjer 2026. Training Dynamics Impact Post-Training Quantization Robustness
- File: `papers/Catalan-Tatjer-2026-Training-Dynamics-PTQ-Robustness.pdf`
- Venue: Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2026 (arXiv:2510.06213v2)
- Does: Measures 3/4-bit GPTQ error (relative cross-entropy increase, plus 12-task 5-shot accuracy) across hundreds of intermediate checkpoints of OLMo, OLMo2 (1B–32B, 4–6T tokens), SmolLM3 (3B, 11T), Apertus (8B, 15T), Open-science and Amber, then trains 70M/160M models on FineWebEdu up to 100B tokens varying LR, schedule, weight decay and weight averaging, and estimates Hessian sharpness/trace.
- Finds: Quantization error is flat through the constant-LR phase and spikes when the learning rate decays, regardless of tokens seen; re-running Kumar's sweep with a WSD schedule removes most of the data-scaling trend at 160M. Higher peak LR gives lower error at equal validation loss, weight averaging (LAWA, model soups) beats LR decay for 3-bit error, and sharpness/trace rise during decay, pointing to flatter minima as the mechanism.
- Use here: Experimental hygiene for the from-scratch GPT-2: compare languages only at matched points in the LR schedule (after decay) and treat the schedule, not token count, as the primary confound; background for interpreting Kumar/Ouyang.
- Note: Mostly GPTQ (AWQ/BNB in appendix); loss-landscape and curvature evidence is correlational; downstream accuracy shown only for SmolLM3.

### Williams 2024. On the Impact of Calibration Data in Post-training Quantization and Pruning
- File: `papers/Williams-2024-Calibration-Data-Pruning-Quantization.pdf`
- Venue: NAACL 2024 (ACL Anthology; the PDF is arXiv:2311.09755 v2, no venue printed)
- Does: Compresses LLaMA, Vicuna and OPT (7B–33B) with GPTQ and SpQR (4-bit) and SparseGPT and Wanda (2:4), varying only the calibration data: five English sources (C4, CNN-DM, RedPajama, RefinedWeb, Wikipedia) x ten random 128x2048-token sets, 1,800 models evaluated on 10 zero-shot tasks plus WikiText perplexity.
- Finds: Quantization is much less calibration-sensitive than pruning: across all 50 sets the mean zero-shot accuracy range is 0.9–1.6 points for GPTQ and 0.6–1.0 for SpQR versus 2.4–4.8 for SparseGPT, though single tasks (BoolQ, RTE) swing several points; source-dataset differences are ~0.2 points for SpQR; perplexity plateaus by ~128 examples (GPTQ 6.13 -> 5.90); perplexity spread understates downstream spread.
- Use here: The English-only baseline for E3: establishes how much calibration data matters at all and that reporting must average over several calibration draws; the project extends the same design to LRL calibration sets, which the authors list as future work.
- Note: English only, 4-bit only, no activation quantization (SmoothQuant discussed but not run); they cite Zeng et al. 2024 (multilingual calibration sampling) as related.

### Timkey 2021. All Bark and No Bite: Rogue Dimensions in Transformer Language Models Obscure Representational Quality
- File: `papers/Timkey-vanSchijndel-2021-All-Bark-No-Bite-Rogue-Dimensions.pdf`
- Venue: EMNLP 2021 (ACL Anthology; the PDF is arXiv:2109.04404, no venue printed)
- Does: Decomposes cosine similarity and Euclidean distance by dimension in every layer of BERT, RoBERTa, GPT-2 and XLNet (768-d) on English Wikipedia, ablates single dimensions and measures the KL change in the LM distribution, and tests post-processing against four human word-similarity datasets.
- Finds: 1–5 "rogue" dimensions with means far from zero and very high variance dominate similarity (one XLNet dimension carries >99% of expected cosine; removing one dimension leaves r^2 = 0.03 for XLNet-12, 0.05 for BERT-11) and are tied to "." and position 0, yet ablating them changes model behaviour very little in GPT-2/XLNet. Standardization restores correlation with human judgments in nearly all layers and beats all-but-the-top and mean-subtraction alone.
- Use here: Recommended procedure before any residual-stream norm, activation-statistics or cosine-based comparison across languages: z-score each dimension against corpus statistics, i.e. compute the mean vector mu = (1/|O|) sum x and per-dimension std sigma over a token corpus O for that layer and use z = (x − mu)/sigma; otherwise per-language "norm" differences may reflect a couple of massive dimensions (relevant to E3 and to the language-specific-dimension analysis).
- Note: GPT-2-era 12-layer models only; corpus statistics must be recomputed per model/layer (and here per language); no confidence intervals in the tables.

### Zhong 2025. Language Lives in Sparse Dimensions: Toward Interpretable and Efficient Multilingual Control for Large Language Models
- File: `papers/Zhong-2025-Language-Lives-in-Sparse-Dimensions.pdf`
- Venue: preprint, arXiv:2510.07213 (v2, 29 Jan 2026); no venue printed anywhere in the PDF
- Does: On Llama2-7B/13B, Llama3.1-8B and Aya23-8B, identifies language-specific dimensions for fr, de, es, zh, ja as the top-K (K=400, ~8–10% of hidden size) dimensions with the largest absolute difference between corpus mean vectors, either intermediate layer (20/22) vs final layer on ~50 monolingual sentences or English vs target final-layer means on 50 FLORES parallel pairs; at inference it overwrites those dimensions at one layer with alpha times the target-language mean.
- Finds: The intervention switches output language while keeping content: ACC*BLEU 16.63 on Llama2-7B (monolingual setting) vs 7.76 for Neuron-Kojima and 15.14 for Neuron-Tang, and ~19 on Llama3.1-8B; the dimensions are shared across layers (later layers need larger alpha because magnitudes grow) and partly across languages (zh/ja share 193 of 400, fr/es 152); 50 sentences are enough, more data does not help.
- Use here: Defines the "language-specific dimensions" measurement for the project: compute the top-K mean-difference dimensions for sw/yo/zu vs en, then test whether per-module quantization error and AWQ/SmoothQuant scales (fit on English calibration data) disproportionately hit those dimensions (E3).
- Note: Filename year 2025 vs v2 dated 2026; only high/mid-resource languages, none of sw/yo/zu; results averaged over 3 seeds for dimension sampling; hyperparameters tuned on Llama2-7B and transferred, so the Llama3.1 parallel-setting result is weak.

### Dumas 2025. Separating Tongue from Thought: Activation Patching Reveals Language-Agnostic Concept Representations in Transformers
- File: `papers/Dumas-2025-Separating-Tongue-From-Thought.pdf`
- Venue: Proceedings of the 63rd Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics (Volume 1: Long Papers), ACL 2025, pages 31822–31841 (earlier version at the ICML 2024 Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop)
- Does: Runs activation patching on Llama 2 7B (checked on Llama 2 70B, Llama 3 8B, Mistral 7B, Qwen 1.5 7B, Aya 23 8B, Gemma 2 2B) with 5-shot single-word translation prompts: the residual stream of the last token is copied from a source prompt (different concept, input and output language) into a target prompt at layer j, and the probability of each concept-in-language is read off, using BabelNet for all valid surface forms.
- Finds: In Llama 2 7B the output language is fixed in the residual stream by about layer 12 and the concept by about layer 16 of 32 (patching layers 0–11 -> target concept in target language, 12–16 -> target concept in source language, 16–31 -> source concept in source language). Patching the mean latent of a concept across several source languages keeps or raises the correct-translation probability, and the model can write definitions from such mean latents, supporting language-agnostic concept representations (means and 95% CIs over 200 prompt pairs).
- Use here: Provides the patching protocol and the layer split (early "language" layers vs mid "concept" layers vs late token-space layers) for the project's activation-patching and logit-lens work: test whether LRL quantization damage concentrates in the language-specific late layers or in the shared concept layers, which discriminates E3/E4 from E1/E2.
- Note: Word-level translation only, with mostly high-resource languages (de, fr, it, zh, es, ru, nl, ko, fi); no quantization involved, so it is a method source, not evidence about PTQ.
